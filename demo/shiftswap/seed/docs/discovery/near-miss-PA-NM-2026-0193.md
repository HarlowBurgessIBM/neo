# Near-miss summary — PA-NM-2026-0193

> **Fictional demo content.** The event, the people, and the refinery were invented for the
> ShiftSwap demonstration.

| | |

| Event date | 2026-09-17 |
| Unit | Hydrocracker Unit, console |
| Classification | Near miss — no injury, no loss of containment |
| Investigation lead | Simone Arceneaux, HSSE Manager |
| Summary issued | 2026-10-02 |

## What happened

A console operator worked a Crude Distillation Unit day shift that ended at 18:00 and was held
over until 19:30 to complete a handover. Under a swap approved two days earlier, the same operator
started a Hydrocracker Unit day shift at 05:00 the next morning. The off-duty period was 9 hours
30 minutes.

At 09:40 the operator acknowledged a high-temperature alarm on the reactor effluent and did not
act on it for 11 minutes. The field operator noticed the trend and raised it by radio. The unit
was brought back within limits with no trip.

## Findings

1. The swap was approved by text message. The approving supervisor did not have the other unit's
   roster to hand and did not calculate the off-duty period.
2. As scheduled, the swap left 11 hours off duty, which meets VE-HSSE-STD-014 § 3.1 and requires
   supervisor review under § 4.2(a). The holdover reduced it to 9 hours 30 minutes.
3. There is no record of who approved the swap or what was checked. The spreadsheet entry shows
   only the two names and the date.

## Recommendations

1. Check the off-duty period, the seven-day hours, and the qualification of both employees before
   a swap is approved, not after it is worked.
2. Record every swap decision as VE-HSSE-STD-014 § 4.5 requires.
3. Review separately how holdovers are controlled. That is outside the swap process.
