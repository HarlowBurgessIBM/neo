# Discovery notes — supervisor and operator interviews, and the four-week tally

> **Fictional demo content.** The people, the refinery, and every figure here were invented for the
> ShiftSwap demonstration.

## 1. Four-week tally

Both supervisors kept a tally sheet for every swap request they received during the period. HSSE
and payroll then reviewed the worked swaps afterwards.

| Measure | Count |
| --- | --- |
| Swap requests received | 46 |
| Approved | 41 |
| Declined | 5 |
| Approved swaps later found to leave an off-duty period under 11 hours | 3 |
| Approved swaps later found to put an operator on a unit or role without a current qualification | 1 |
| Approved swaps not entered in the scheduling system before the shift was worked | 7 |

Supervisor handling time per request, from the same tally sheets (minutes from reading the request
to recording a decision, including time spent checking the roster):

| | Minutes |
| --- | --- |
| Median | 14 |
| Shortest | 4 |
| Longest | 55 |

The tally was self-reported by two supervisors. It was not timed by an observer.

## 2. Renee Landry — shift supervisor, Crude Distillation Unit

- "The request comes in as a text while I'm on the unit. I check the roster printout when I get
  back to the desk, and by then I've forgotten who asked."
- "I don't calculate rest hours. I look at whether they're coming off nights. If it looks fine,
  I approve it."
- "The ones that worry me are the cross-unit swaps. Hydrocracker changes shift an hour before we
  do, and I can't see their roster."
- "Most swaps are nothing. Same unit, same shift, a few days apart. I shouldn't have to touch
  those at all."
- On what counts toward hours: "Training days count. If someone's in the simulator for ten hours,
  that's ten hours."

## 3. Carl Nguyen — shift supervisor, Hydrocracker Unit

- "I approve it, then I'm supposed to email the scheduler. Sometimes that happens two days later."
- "I have no way of knowing someone's console qualification lapsed unless training tells me."
- "If a swap is refused, the operator wants to know which rule. 'No' isn't good enough, and it
  shouldn't be."
- On what counts toward hours: "The standard says scheduled shifts. Training isn't on the roster,
  so I don't count it."

## 4. Two console operators (interviewed together, names withheld at their request)

- "We want an answer before we leave shift, not the next day."
- "Half the time we don't know if it was approved, so both of us turn up, or neither."
- "I'd rather be told no straight away with the reason than find out it was a problem later."

## 5. Open points raised in the interviews

- The two supervisors disagree on whether training days count toward the hours limit (§ 2 and § 3
  above). The fatigue standard defines scheduled hours by shifts on the published roster and does
  not mention training. Nobody interviewed could say which reading HSSE intends.
- Neither supervisor could say how many swap requests arrive around a shift change. The tally
  recorded dates, not times.
