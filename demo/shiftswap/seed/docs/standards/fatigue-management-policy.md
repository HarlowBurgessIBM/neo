## 1. Purpose

This standard sets the limits on shift work that protect operations personnel from fatigue, and
the conditions under which two employees may swap shifts.

## 2. Definitions

- **Shift** — a scheduled period of work with a start and an end, for one unit and one role.
- **Published roster** — the roster exported from the site scheduling system. The scheduling
  system is the system of record for who is scheduled to work.
- **Site time** — local time at Port Alder Refinery (IANA time zone `America/Chicago`).
- **Elapsed time** — actual hours passed, not the difference between two clock readings. On the
  two nights each year when site clocks change, the two differ by one hour.
- **Off-duty period** — the elapsed time between the end of one of an employee's shifts and the
  start of that employee's next shift.
- **Scheduled hours** — the elapsed time between a shift's start and its end.
- **Qualification** — a record that an employee is current for one role on one unit, with a
  *valid through* date. The qualification is valid on that date and expires the day after.

## 3. Limits

### 3.1 Minimum off-duty period

An employee shall have an off-duty period of at least **11 hours** between consecutive shifts.

### 3.2 Hours in seven days

An employee's scheduled hours, counted over the shifts that start within any **7 consecutive
calendar days** (site-time dates), shall not exceed **72 hours**.

### 3.3 Qualification

An employee shall work a shift only if they hold a qualification for that shift's unit and role
that is valid on the site-time date the shift starts.

## 4. Shift swaps

### 4.1 What a swap is

A swap exchanges one scheduled shift of one employee for one scheduled shift of another. Both
employees must agree to it. A swap is permitted only if, for **each** of the two employees, the
resulting schedule in the published roster meets every limit in § 3.

Handing a shift to another employee without taking one in return is not a swap. The scheduler
handles it and it is outside this section.

### 4.2 Supervisor review

A swap that meets § 3 requires review and approval by the shift supervisor of each unit affected
when, for either employee, the resulting schedule has:

- (a) an off-duty period of less than **12 hours**; or
- (b) more than **60 scheduled hours** in any 7 consecutive calendar days.

### 4.3 Swaps that need no review

A swap that meets § 3 and triggers neither condition in § 4.2 may be approved without supervisor
review.

### 4.4 No exceptions by swap

A swap that does not meet § 3 shall not be approved. Exceptions to § 3 are granted only through
the fatigue exception procedure (VE-HSSE-PRC-021), which is separate from the swap process.

### 4.5 Records

Every swap decision shall be recorded with: the two employees and the two shifts; the outcome;
each limit in §§ 3–4 that determined the outcome; who decided and when; and the revision of this
standard that was applied. Records are retained for three years.

## 5. Relationship to other standards

Veridane states that its fatigue risk management system is aligned with ANSI/API Recommended
Practice 755. This document does not reproduce that recommended practice. Where this standard is
stricter than RP 755, or addresses something RP 755 does not, this standard governs at Port Alder.
